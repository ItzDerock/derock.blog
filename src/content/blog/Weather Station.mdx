Weather is very hard to predict, being known as a "chaotic system," where even
the smallest unmeasured wind gust can throw off the entire model. Just like any
prediction problem, the more data one can collect, the more accurate the
predictions will be. Through services like Weather Underground, you can link
your own Personal Weather Station (PWS) to a larger array of other PWSes and
improve prediction accuracy for everyone. Furthermore, you can get precise,
localized, current weather information for where the weather station gets
deployed.

### Pressure

Since the previous sensor does not provide pressure reading, we purchased the
**[LPS25](link)** for that job, which has an accuracy of /_ TODO _/. Pressure
drops is a really good indicator of a forming storm and other weather
conditions.

The "Secret Law of Storms" shows:

$$
\frac{dv}{dt} = - \frac{22.3}{v^{0.553}} \times \frac{dp}{dt} \\
$$

- where $\frac{dv}{dt}$ is wind acceleration (velocity derivative with respect
  to time)
- and $\frac{dp}{dt}$ is the pressure rate of change

In layman's terms,

> At a given wind speed, the marginal rate of increase of sustained wind speed
> is directly proportional to the marginal rate of pressure fall.

Source:
[worldstormcentral.co](https://www.worldstormcentral.co/law%20of%20storms/secret%20law%20of%20storms.html)

This pressure drop, along with humidity changes is something we humans
subconsciously pick up on, and is why you can feel that a storm is coming.

### UV Index

Many UV index sensors estimate the UV index bsaed on light levels, without
actually measuring UVA/UVB levels. For simplicity, we only need to focus on
UVA/UVB measurement, and can omit UVC since that gets largely absorbed by the
atmosphere.

We considered the following sensors,

- **VEML6075**
  - Measures UVB (315nm-340nm), and UVA (350nm-375nm) for originally under $10.
  - Except, this sensor is discontinued, with one user reporting: "I have tested
    the VEML 6075 sensor and found it utterly unreliable, delivering data which
    did not make much sense. There is probably a good reason for the
    manufacturer having stopped production!" So we decided against purchasing
    this.
- **AS7331**
  - Measures UVA (320-400nm), UVB (280-320nm), and UVC (200-280nm) with high
    accuracy.
  - But, it is more expensive than others: $27.50
- **LTR390**
  - Measures both ambient light and UVA with peak between 300 and 350nm.
  - Has an easy-to-read UV index register, and the manufacturer datasheet shows
    high accuracy; an R^2 of 0.9843. /_ TODO: graph _/
  - **Decided to purchase this sensor**

### CO2 Sensor

Despite how the media portrays it, outdoor CO2 levels aren't high enough for
this to be a major factor in the air quality. /_fact_/

Furthermore, many CO2 sensors _estimate_ the CO2 levels based on VOC gas
concentration or some other correlated variable and do not actually measure the
true CO2 concentration. Many sensors also perform a "baseline calibration" where
it will assume the lowest reading in a certain time period (i.e. 24hr) is 400ppm
and all other measurements are relative to that.

**The following sensors were considered, but ultimately omitted:**

| Sensor                 | Rated Accuracy              | Reason for omittence                                                                                |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| MH-Z19C                | 50 ppm +- 5% of measurement | Has automatic baseline calibration                                                                  |
| SCD-40                 | +-(50 ppm + 5% of reading)  | Inaccurate, outdoor ppm will only vary by 50-100ppm, so it will be hard to separate error from data |
| Senseair S8 004-0-0053 | +- 3%                       | Cannot use in cold temperatures, rated operating range is 0C to 50C                                 |
| CM1106SL               | +-(50 ppm + 5% of reading)  | Has automatic baseline calibration, but can be disabled. Is also pretty inaccurate for outdoor use  |
| SDC-30                 | +-(30ppm + 3%)              | Slightly more accurate than others, but at a high cost: $58.95                                      |
